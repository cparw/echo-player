Their formula (same in all four):

3. Captions are key phrases, not the full script: they build 1 to 3 words at a time, up to about 6 words on 2 or 3 lines, white italic caps with a shadow and no box, key words green, danger words red, placed centre to lower centre.
4. Numbered or rule by rule structure. Each item gets a small top header ("1. GUNS") with an emoji. "GTA 5 did X, GTA 6 does Y" contrasts, labelled with the two game logos.
5. One custom "game HUD" graphic per beat (star bar, heat area map, clue icons, sliders, meters), in dark boxes with a green outline, synced to the voice. This is what makes it feel like an official guide.
6. No end card and no spoken call to action. The last words ("And in case you missed it...") are cut mid sentence over the opening shot, so the reel loops into its first line. Two reels ask a question first ("Which one are you most hyped for?").
7. One fast, upbeat American male narrator (probably AI), with a constant low bed under the voice. Post caption is just the title and 2 or 3 hashtags.

Why the last two took off (our read): one clear promise ("how X works", "everything you need to know before you play") instead of a generic list; a strong first frame; a graphic for every claim; captions the whole way through; the police system is also the top feature in `features_that_hit.md` (9.5).

Do NOT copy: Rockstar's logo on screen; guns, robberies, shooting at police, sexualised shots; fan made HUD and AI or unknown clips passed off as real gameplay; mechanics stated as fact without a source (several echo the 2022 leaks); their exact lines and their green italic look.
